Token Design &amp; Blockchain Fundamentals                  Q1. What is a fungible token?                  A fungible token is interchangeable with any other unit of the same type.                  Example: ERC-20 tokens like USDC — every 1 USDC is identical.                  Problem for carbon credits:                 Fungibility hides origin, vintage, methodology, and usage history.                                     Q2. What is a semi-fungible token?                  A semi-fungible token is:                      fungible within a batch (same project, vintage, methodology),                      non-fungible across batches or over time.                  In this system:                      credits from the same issuance batch trade interchangeably,                      but remain serialized, traceable, and sliceable.                  This enables partial retirement, PAYG, auditability, and liquidity.                                     Q3. Why not use fully non-fungible tokens (NFTs)?                  Pure NFTs:                      are inefficient for bulk trading,                      make fractional usage expensive,                      increase gas costs and fragmentation.                   Semi-fungible tokens (ERC-1155 / ERC-3525) balance:                      liquidity,                      granularity,                      traceability.                                     Q4. What is serialization or a serialization range?                  Serialization assigns unique identifiers to each unit (or slice) of carbon credit.                  Example:                  Serialized range: 0 – 1,000,000                  Each serial = 1 tCO₂e                  This enables:                      precise ownership,                      exact retirement,                      non-reusability of credits.                                     Q5. Why do serialization ranges need to be locked?                  Locking prevents:                      duplicate issuance,                      replay attacks,                      double counting.                  Lifecycle:                      Unlocked → Minted → Locked                      Locked → Burned → Permanently retired                  There is no valid state path for reuse.                                     B. Market &amp; Trading Concepts                  Q6. What is OTC trading?                  OTC (Over-The-Counter) trading is:                      bilateral,                       privately negotiated,                      often broker-mediated.                  Traditional carbon markets rely heavily on OTC, causing:                      high costs,                      low transparency,                      slow settlement.                                     Q7. What is an order book?                  An order book matches:                      buy orders (bids),                      sell orders (asks),based on price and quantity.                  Common in stock and commodity exchanges.                                     Q8. What is an AMM (Automated Market Maker)?                  An AMM is a smart contract that:                      provides liquidity automatically,                      replaces buyers/sellers matching with a pricing formula.                  No brokers, no intermediaries.                                     Q9. What is a constant-product AMM?                  A common AMM pricing rule:                  x × y = k                  Where:                      x = credit reserve,                      y = stablecoin reserve,                      k = constant.                  Buying one asset automatically adjusts price based on scarcity.                                     Q10. Why use AMMs for carbon credits?                   AMMs:                      reduce transaction costs,                      ensure continuous liquidity,                      support retail PAYG use cases,                      discourage hoarding by enabling easy exit.                                     C. Carbon Market Problems &amp; Concepts                  Q11. What is hoarding in carbon credits?                  Hoarding is buying credits and holding them indefinitely without retiring them.                  Example:         A trader buys credits in 2025 and holds them until 2035, never offsetting emissions.                  Problems:                      artificial scarcity,                      price manipulation,                      zero climate impact.                                     Q12. How does this system prevent hoarding?                  Through:                      expiry / decay rules,                      AMM liquidity pressure,                      PAYG micro-retirement,                      enforced PoO for claims.                  Hoarding becomes economically irrational.                                     Q13. What is greenwashing?                  Greenwashing is claiming environmental benefits without real impact.                  Examples:                      claiming “carbon neutral” without retiring credits,                      using low-quality or double-counted offsets.                                      Q14. How does the system prevent greenwashing?                  By enforcing:                      Proof-of-Integrity (PoI) before minting,                      Proof-of-Offset (PoO) at retirement,                      serialized, auditable receipts.                  Claims become cryptographically verifiable, not marketing statements.                                     Q15. What is double counting?                  Double counting occurs when the same emissions reduction is claimed more than once.                  Example:                      a country claims reductions for national targets,                      a company claims the same reduction for ESG reporting.                                     Q16. How is double counting prevented?                  By construction:                      serialization,                      bounded minting,                      exact serial burning,                      unique PoO issuance.                  There is no valid path to claim the same serial twice.                                     D. MRV, Auditing &amp; Data Integrity                  Q17. What is MRV?                  MRV = Measurement, Reporting, Verification                  It ensures emissions reductions are:                      accurately measured,                      consistently reported,                      independently verified.                                      Q18. Why keep MRV data off-chain?                  Because raw MRV data is:                      large,                      sensitive,                      expensive to store on-chain.                  Instead:                      raw data stays off-chain,                      hashes + Merkle roots anchor integrity on-chain.                                     Q19. What is a Merkle root and why use it?                  A Merkle root is a single hash representing many data points.                  Benefits:                      tamper evidence,                      efficient verification,                      constant-time integrity checks.                                     Q20. What is a Zero-Knowledge Proof (ZKP)?                  A ZKP proves:                  “Valid data satisfies condition X”                 without revealing the data itself.                                     Q21. Why use ZKPs in carbon credits?                  To:                      protect proprietary industrial data,                      preserve confidentiality,                      still enforce trustless verification.                                     Q22. Why not rely on AI-only MRV verification?                  AI:                      detects anomalies,                       does not prove correctness.                  ZKPs:                      provide mathematical guarantees.                  This system uses AI for detection, ZKPs for enforcement.                                     E. Two-Proof Model (Core Contribution)                  Q23. What is Proof-of-Integrity (PoI)?                  PoI proves:                  “This credit is legitimate and allowed to exist.”                  It is:                      generated by accredited VVBs,                      ZKP-verified,                      mandatory before minting.                                     Q24. What is Proof-of-Offset (PoO)?                  PoO proves:                  “This credit has been permanently used.”                  It is:                      generated at retirement,                      non-transferable,                      the only valid offset claim.                                     Q25. Why separate PoI and PoO?                  Because:                      legitimacy ≠ consumption,                      issuance ≠ settlement.                  This separation removes:                      zombie credits,                       lifecycle ambiguity,                      double claims.                                     Q26. Why is PoO a separate object instead of a flag?                  A flag:                      disappears with state changes,                      cannot be exported,                      lacks evidentiary value.                  PoO:                      is a cryptographic receipt,                      is portable,                      persists forever.                                     Q27. Why is PoO non-transferable?                  Because offset claims:                      represent responsibility,                      must be unique,                      must not be traded.                  Transferable PoO would reintroduce double-claim risk.                                     F. Registry, Governance &amp; Trust                  Q28. Why is the registry superior to markets?                  Markets:                      discover price.                  Registry:                      controls existence,                      enforces lifecycle,                      guarantees integrity.                  This prevents market manipulation of validity.                                      Q29. Isn’t the registry centralized?                  No discretionary power exists.                  All actions are:                      rule-based,                      cryptographically enforced,                      publicly auditable.                  Power is constrained by code, not operators.                                     Q30. What happens if a VVB misbehaves?                  Mitigations:                      whitelisting,                      on-chain revocation,                      PoI invalidation,                      audit trails and slashing (optional).                                     Q31. Can governments or regulators verify this system?                  Yes.                  They can:                      run read-only nodes,                      verify PoI and PoO,                      audit claims without accessing raw data.                                     Q32. How are methodologies updated over time?                  Methodologies are:                      versioned,                      whitelisted,                      locked per project at registration.                  No retroactive rule changes.                                     G. Lifecycle, Risk &amp; System Behavior                   Q33. Why enforce a strict lifecycle?                  Without it:                      credits float indefinitely,                      responsibility blurs,                      claims lose meaning.                  Lifecycle enforced:                  Existence → Trade → Consumption → Settlement                                     Q34. What happens when credits expire?                  Expired credits:                      cannot be traded,                      cannot be retired,                      lose economic value.                  This enforces timely use.                                     Q35. Why allow partial (fractional) retirement?                  Because emissions occur continuously.                  Enables:                      PAYG,                      IoT-linked offsets,                      real-time settlement.                                     Q36. What if MRV data is challenged after minting?                  Serialization allows:                      targeted invalidation,                      freezing affected batches,                      avoiding system-wide rollback.                                     Q37. What if sensors fail or produce noise?                   Handled via:                      aggregation windows,                      uncertainty buffers,                      conservative issuance bounds.                  Over-crediting is structurally prevented.                                     Q38. Can credits be bridged across blockchains?                  Yes, via wrapped credits that preserve:                      serialization,                      PoI/PoO references,                      registry authority.                  Ownership moves, authority does not.                                     Q39. How does the system scale to billions of micro-offsets?                  Via:                      batch serialization,                      range-based PoO,                      constant-size ZK proofs,                      L2 settlement.                                     H. Big-Picture &amp; Conceptual Questions                  Q40. Is this a carbon market or a carbon settlement system?                  It is a carbon settlement protocol.                  Markets are a side effect.                 Settlement is the purpose.                                     Q41. How is this different from existing systems?                  Existing systems:                      track credits.                  This system:                       cryptographically settles carbon liabilities.                                     Q42. What happens if the platform disappears?                  PoI and PoO:                      are on-chain,                      independently verifiable,                      persist without the operator.                  Claims remain valid forever.                                     Q43. Who benefits most from this system?                      Corporates (verifiable ESG),                      Retail users (PAYG),                      Regulators (audit-grade trails),                      Developers (liquidity),                      Climate (real offsets).                                     Q44. What is the strongest claim you can make?                  It is impossible, not just unlikely, to mint, trade, or claim carbon credits dishonestly within this protocol.                                     Q45. One-sentence technical summary?                  A registry-first, proof-driven carbon settlement protocol that cryptographically separates credit legitimacy from consumption using ZK-enforced issuance and serialized retirement.
