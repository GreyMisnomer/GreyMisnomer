Background                             The UNFCCC (United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change) formalized Clean Development Mechanism (CDM) under the Kyoto Protocol in 1997. Carbon credits emerged as an economic instrument to price carbon emissions and address global warming through economic mechanisms rather than regulation. Policymakers introduced cap-and-trade and offset mechanisms so emissions reductions could be quantified, verified, and converted into tradable units. The idea was One carbon credit          equal to          1 tonne of CO₂-equivalent reduced, avoided, or removed.                             During the expansion of voluntary era (post 2010) companies like Delta         ,          Disney bought Voluntary Credits to look good (CSR) and phantom credits were sold for forests that were never in danger, or re-sold multiple times. In CDM, credits were treated as certificates, not assets which has reliance on manual audits with high costs, slow issuance, broker dominance and bureaucratic. Post-Kyoto the demand collapsed, prices were crashed and small players got excluded.                             Standards like Verra, Gold Standard (GS) emerged for corporate CSR digitized the trading, nature-based credits (forestry) surged.                   The market was plagued by fragmented registries, phantom credits (issued for non-existent reductions) and double-counting (same credit claimed by multiple entities).          At 2015 world agreed under Paris Agreement (Article 6) that carbon trading must be high integrity and every tonne must be accounted for. The focus shifts on integrity, transparency, and avoidance of double counting. Now         ,          carbon credits are becoming regulated financial environmental instruments with infrastructure and not just markets.                   Infrastructure to verify small, decentralized projects cheaply and securely does not exist.                             India is the 3          rd          largest emitter but also has the biggest potential for low-cost reduction, 2023 onwards India officially announce Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (CCTS) and its own domestic carbon market (ICM). India          announced          a domestic, sovereign carbon market under law, Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act, 2022, Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (CCTS), 2023 with compliance-first market, domestic use priority, government-controlled registry as key characteristic         s         . Now, a farmer planting trees can sell credits to a Steel Plant that pollutes, legal framework exists, but the technology to verify millions of small farmers cheaply does not.                             This creates need for a new Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI) for carbon credits capable of programmatic verification, cryptographic integrity, and lifecycle enforcement, ensure privacy and scale to meet the demands of such economy.                                    2           .
